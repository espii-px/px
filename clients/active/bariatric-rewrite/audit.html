The goal of our audit

What gets a business from 0 to 1 is not what takes it from 1 to 100. Getting to 100 takes a different level of sophistication, systems and data intelligence.

Your goals tell you where to go. Your systems decide whether you get there. You need two:

  1. A fulfilment system that creates amazing transformations.
  2. An acquisition system that consistently creates demand.

When we analyse your data, we want to understand five things:

  1. What is working to get sales, and why it works.
  2. How we double down on what works and cut what doesn't.
  3. The low-hanging fruit and quick wins.
  4. Which foundations need to be built to help you scale.
  5. The action plan: what the next 90 to 180 days look like to close the gap to the goal.
PX mental models
  • Marketing = (psychology + economics) x technology
  • Data = collection, analysis, interpretation, action
  • Build foundations = Standardise > Optimise > Scale, or your problems scale with you
Overview of findings
  • The goal: a $1M a month acquisition channel.
  • Today: $75,881 of Meta spend from 1 Jan to 30 Sep 2026, about $8,400 a month. At best that bought 87 sales, $872 each.
  • The gap: 71% of spend goes to Messenger chats that can't be tied to a sale, and nothing retargets the people who don't book.
  • The plan: simplify to three cash cow top-of-funnel paths (Facebook group with a monthly workshop, the app, the workshop), make every one trackable, and layer a book-a-call funnel on top as retargeting.

Positioning
The problem, explained better than she can

Surgery and GLP-1s change the body, not the habits and coping patterns built over a lifetime. So she can know exactly what to eat and still graze at 9pm. The gap is not knowledge. It is consistency, with no one in her corner once the surgical team moves on.

Mechanism, enemy and new way

The Bariatric Rewrite Method: Recognize the habits and triggers that keep you stuck. Rewrite them with practical nutrition, coaching and daily feedback. Reinforce them with ongoing accountability and a community who have had the surgery.

What's broken
  • The ads buy chats, not sales. Chats never reach the pixel, so Meta has no idea who applied, booked or bought, and keeps finding more people who like to chat.
  • Messenger results are hidden, not missing. Setters route each chat to the booking link, a workshop or the app, under one tag (appsent). 3,271 Meta ad leads got that tag: 1,259 booked, 855 showed and 83 bought (2.5%). We can't see which route closes.
  • Contacts split in two. The Messenger ad creates a contact with no email. The person later books under a second contact from the survey, the app or a workshop, so Messenger's sales land under the wrong source.
  • App ads are told to find the wrong people. They optimize for page views, not sign-ups. The page converts 2.5% of visitors and sends nothing back to Meta.
  • Budget spread too thin. 50 of 69 ad sets spend under $10 a day, too little for Meta to learn.
  • Nobody is retargeted. No retargeting layer, no workshop replay follow-up, no ads to people who booked or cancelled.
What's working
  • Workshop ads bring the cheapest leads in the account: 119 leads at $1.40 in September, 198 at $2.52 in January. Both campaigns were switched off.
  • Something inside Messenger works. Setters do route people to booking, workshops and the app, and some of those routes sell. It needs tracking, not cutting.
  • The audience is already paid for. Ten months of video viewers, chatters, app users and group members are ready to be built into retargeting pools.
What we suggest

No ad sends a cold lead straight to a call. Every prospecting ad sends people into a warm-up, and everyone who warms up is retargeted to the 1:1 consult.

What's not, and the gaps
Chats are not sales. Meta ad leads overall buy at 0.55%. A cold ad straight to a call closes 2% (2 of 106). Comment-for-a-guide leads buy at 2.6% (7 of 269).
  • Landing page to call ads cost $111 per lead. The LPBC campaign spent $14,514 for 131 leads.
  • App install ads bought installs, not leads. $6,350 bought about 3,880 installs and 31 tracked leads. Sales from those installs (pending).
  • The winners are fatiguing. Ad 05 costs $2.44 per chat in the last 30 days against $1.58 lifetime. Ad 16 costs $1.72 against $1.10. Both launched 1 Nov 2025.
  • 71% of spend is in a channel we don't run. $53,685 (Jan to Sep 2026) went to the third-party Messenger ads, and their chat never answers its own top question: "What's the investment?"

Source: Meta Ads Manager; 1 Oct audit

Ad 16. Hook calls out regain after surgery. Kills two old fixes: "I wouldn't add more protein shakes" and "I wouldn't do a pouch reset". Reframe: "Regain isn't a failure. It's a signal." Bridge: structure, education, support. CTA: "Drop REWRITE". It works because it names what she was about to try next.

Ad 15. Hook quotes her own self-talk. Founder admits she said it too. Reframe: "It's not a cure. It's not magic. It's a tool." CTA: "Drop REWRITE". The steadiest winner: $1.25 per chat in the last 30 days.

Ad 05. Hook promises a fight. Reframe: "This surgery isn't a diet. It's a medical treatment for a disease." It calls out people "still arguing carbs vs keto 3 years out". Most watched of the three, and fatiguing fastest.

Best structure
  1. Call out and provoke. Name the bariatric patient and the regain, or quote her own words back to her.
  2. Kill the old way. Name the fix she is about to try: shakes, pouch reset, keto arguments.
  3. Reframe the blame. "You didn't fail surgery." Regain is a signal.
  4. Name what's missing. Structure, education, support, from someone who has had the surgery.
  5. Bridge in one line. "That's what we do at The Bariatric Rewrite."
  6. Low-friction CTA. Comment or message one word.

Open questions for the call
